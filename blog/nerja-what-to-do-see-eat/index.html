Nerja: What to Do, See, and Eat in 2026

Malaga VIP Transfers•Destination Guide•8 min read

Nerja is one of the last genuinely charming towns on the Costa del Sol — an authentic whitewashed village perched dramatically above the sea, with cove beaches accessible only by boat, the world-famous Cuevas de Nerja, and the iconic Balcón de Europa viewpoint drawing visitors from across Europe. This guide tells you exactly what to do, where to go, and what to eat — and how to get there comfortably from Malaga Airport.

Top Things to Do in Nerja

1. Balcón de Europa

The Balcón de Europa — literally "Balcony of Europe" — is Nerja's defining landmark: a clifftop promenade above the sea where a former fortress once stood. King Alfonso XII gave the viewpoint its name in 1885, and the views across the Mediterranean are genuinely breathtaking. In summer the balcony fills with street performers, tourists, and locals; at dawn or dusk, it becomes magical. Entrance is free.

2. Cuevas de Nerja (Nerja Caves)

The Cuevas de Nerja are among the most spectacular cave systems in Europe. Discovered in 1959 by a group of young men from the village, the caves contain prehistoric paintings more than 40,000 years old — which in 2012 were controversially attributed to Neanderthals, a claim that generated worldwide headlines. The caves also contain what is believed to be the world's largest known stalactite column. A legendary annual concert series takes place inside the largest chamber each July. Open daily; book tickets online in advance during summer.

3. Nerja Beaches

Nerja's beaches are more varied and interesting than most Costa del Sol resorts:

  • Burriana Beach: Nerja's largest and most accessible beach — wide, sandy, with excellent beach restaurants and watersports hire. The beach bar El Chiringuito de Burriana is legendary for its paella.
  • Playa Calahonda: A small, sheltered pebble cove directly below the Balcón de Europa — small, intimate, and beautiful.
  • Playa El Salón: Another small beach accessible by steps from the Balcón de Europa — ideal for snorkelling in the clear water.
  • Maro Beach: Located 4 km east of Nerja, this secluded beach sits in a protected natural area with crystal-clear water and a small waterfall nearby. Access by car or taxi.
  • Carabeo Beach: Accessible only by steps, this intimate pebble cove is a local favourite for its calm waters and the classic restaurant above it.

4. Nerja Old Town

The old town of Nerja spreads back from the Balcón de Europa in a network of pedestrian streets. The Tuesday market (Calle Chaparil) is one of the best in the region — fresh produce, local ceramics, and leather goods at reasonable prices. The Church of El Salvador, built over a former mosque, is worth entering for its unusual mixture of Moorish and Baroque architecture.

5. Maro Waterfall & Cliffs

Just east of Nerja, the area around Maro is designated as a natural park. Fresh water springs create small waterfalls that cascade directly onto the beach at Playa de Maro, while the cliffs above are threaded with walking paths. Kayak hire is available from Burriana Beach to explore the otherwise-inaccessible coastal caves and arches to the east.

Where to Eat in Nerja

  • El Chiringuito de Burriana: The legendary beach restaurant at Burriana Beach. Arrive early for a table at lunch; the mixed paella is exceptional. Expect queues in July–August.
  • Restaurante Carabeo: Cliffside dining with one of the most dramatic views in the region. Fresh Mediterranean cuisine — the grilled sea bass is outstanding.
  • Bar-Restaurante El Molino: A Nerja institution in the old town for traditional Andalusian cuisine — oxtail stew, grilled local fish, classic gazpacho.
  • Taberna El Atelier: Creative contemporary tapas in the old town — relaxed, excellent value, great wine list.

Food tip: Nerja's signature dish is espeto de sardinas — fresh sardines grilled over charcoal in half-barrels on the beach. Burriana Beach has several stalls from May to September; arriving at 1:00 PM catches them at their freshest.

When to Visit Nerja

Nerja is enjoyable year-round, but the best times to visit are:

  • May–June: Warm without the August crowds. Beach restaurants are open, temperatures are comfortable for walking.
  • September–October: The sea remains warm from summer, crowds thin, and prices drop considerably.
  • July–August: Peak season — beaches are busy, accommodation prices are high, but the atmosphere is fantastic.
  • Winter (November–March): Nerja is one of the warmest places in Europe in winter (average 17–20°C). Peaceful, local, and excellent for walking the coastal paths.
