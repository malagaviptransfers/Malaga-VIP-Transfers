First-Timer's Guide to Marbella: Where to Stay, Eat & Go

Malaga VIP Transfers•Destination Guide•9 min read

Marbella is the most glamorous town on the Costa del Sol — and one of the most misunderstood. Many first-time visitors expect wall-to-wall nightclubs and brash excess. What they find instead is a layered town with a beautifully preserved Moorish old quarter, outstanding restaurants, pristine beaches, and yes — the extraordinary spectacle of Puerto Banús. This guide tells you exactly where to go, where to eat, and how to get there from Malaga Airport.

Getting to Marbella from Malaga Airport

Marbella is approximately 60 km from Malaga Airport (AGP), a journey of 45–55 minutes by car via the AP-7 motorway. The Three Faces of Marbella

1. Marbella Old Town (Casco Antiguo)

The historic heart of Marbella is one of the Costa del Sol's greatest surprises. Narrow whitewashed streets hung with flowering bougainvillea lead to the Plaza de los Naranjos (Orange Square) — a gorgeous 15th-century square perfect for morning coffee or evening drinks. The old town is largely car-free, easy to walk, and lined with excellent traditional tapas bars. Don't miss the Church of Our Lady of the Incarnation on the main square.

2. The Golden Mile (Milla de Oro)

The stretch of coastline between Marbella and Puerto Banús is known as the Golden Mile — home to some of Europe's most legendary luxury hotels, including the Marbella Club and Puente Romano. The beach here is pristine, the chiringuitos (beach restaurants) are excellent, and the people-watching is extraordinary.

3. Puerto Banús

Puerto Banús is Marbella's marina — and one of the most extraordinary harbours in the world. Superyachts worth tens of millions line the quayside; Ferraris idle along the main drag; designer boutiques (Gucci, Versace, Louis Vuitton) sit alongside beach bars serving ice-cold fino sherry. Puerto Banús divides opinion — some find it thrilling, others overwhelming — but as a one-off spectacle, it is unmissable.

Where to Stay in Marbella

  • Marbella Old Town: Best for culture, walking, and authentic Spanish atmosphere. Boutique hotels and apartments. Ideal for couples and solo travellers.
  • The Golden Mile: Best for the ultimate luxury experience — the Marbella Club, Puente Romano, and Villa Padierna are among Europe's finest resort hotels.
  • Puerto Banús / Nueva Andalucía: Best for beach clubs, marina dining, and nightlife. Wide range of apartments available for families and groups.
  • Elviria: East of Marbella, this quieter neighbourhood has excellent restaurants, calmer beaches, and more residential character — popular with repeat visitors.

Where to Eat in Marbella

  • La Niña del Pisto (Old Town): Excellent modern tapas in a casual, atmospheric setting
  • Puente Romano Beach Restaurant: The benchmark for beachfront dining — elegant, impeccably serviced
  • Nobu Marbella (at the Hard Rock Hotel): World-class Japanese-Peruvian cuisine, the celebrity favourite
  • El Presidente (Nagüeles): A local institution for grilled fish and fried anchovies, beloved by Marbella's non-expat community
  • Dani García Restaurant (Puente Romano): Three Michelin stars — book months in advance

Tip for first-timers: Marbella's restaurant scene is at its best from 9:00 PM onwards. Arriving at 7:30 PM as many northern Europeans do will mean you eat alone in an empty restaurant. Embrace Spanish dining hours for the best atmosphere and service.

Marbella Beaches

Marbella's beaches run the length of the coastline and vary considerably in character:

  • Playa de la Fontanilla: The main town beach, broad and well-serviced with sunbeds and chiringuitos
  • Playa del Cable / Real de Zaragoza: Quieter, more local-feeling beaches west of the centre
  • Nikki Beach / Ocean Club: Marbella's legendary beach clubs — glamorous, pricey, and spectacular
  • Elviria Beach: Wide, less crowded, with excellent beach restaurants — a favourite for families
