Malaga City Guide: Top Things to Do as a First-Time Visitor

Malaga VIP Transfers•Travel Tips•7 min read

Malaga is Spain's fastest-growing city for tourism — and one of the most underrated. While millions of visitors fly through Malaga Airport (AGP) each year on their way to Marbella or Nerja, a growing number are discovering that Malaga city itself deserves a dedicated stay. This first-timer's guide covers the city's top attractions, best restaurants, and neighbourhood highlights.

Getting from Malaga Airport to Malaga City Centre

Malaga Airport to Malaga city centre is approximately 8 km, making it one of the closest city-to-airport connections in Spain. Options include the Cercanías C1 train (12 min, €1.80), a private transfer from Malaga Airport to Malaga Central (15–20 min, from €66), or a standard taxi (15–25 min, €18–25 with supplements). For groups or travellers with heavy luggage, a private transfer is typically the most efficient.

Top Attractions in Malaga

1. La Alcazaba

The Alcazaba is Malaga's most iconic landmark — a breathtaking 11th-century Moorish palace-fortress built into the hillside above the city. The site combines dramatic architecture, beautifully maintained gardens, and panoramic views over the Mediterranean and the Roman Theatre below. Entrance costs approximately €3.50; arrive at opening (9:00 AM) to avoid queues.

2. Picasso Museum Malaga

Pablo Picasso was born in Malaga in 1881, and the Picasso Museum houses one of the world's most significant collections of his work — over 200 paintings, ceramics, sculptures, and drawings donated by his family. Located in the beautifully restored 16th-century Buenavista Palace in the old town. Book tickets in advance, especially in summer.

3. Atarazanas Market

The Atarazanas Central Market is housed in a stunning 14th-century Moorish archway that was once the entrance to the city's naval dockyards. Inside, you'll find an extraordinary range of fresh produce, local olive oil, cured meats, and the finest seafood the Mediterranean can offer. Visit in the morning for the best selection.

4. Malaga Cathedral (La Manquita)

Malaga's Cathedral — known locally as La Manquita (the one-armed lady) because one of its two towers was never completed — is a magnificent Renaissance structure dominating the city skyline. Climb to the roof for one of the best panoramic views in the city.

5. Malaga Beach (La Malagueta)

Malaga city's main beach, La Malagueta, sits just east of the port and is walkable from the city centre. The beach is well-equipped with sunbeds, beach bars, and several good chiringuito restaurants specialising in espeto (charcoal-grilled sardines on skewers).

6. Carmen Thyssen Museum

An excellent collection of 19th-century Spanish and Andalusian painting in a beautiful 16th-century mansion. Far less crowded than the Picasso Museum and often producing the greater surprise.

Malaga Food & Drink

Malaga has developed a genuinely exciting culinary scene without abandoning the tapas tradition that makes the region so loveable:

  • Espetos de Sardinas: The definitive Malaga dish — fresh sardines threaded on skewers and grilled over charcoal on the beach. Available at virtually every beach restaurant.
  • Gazpacho & Ajoblanco: Cold soups that are absolute necessities in summer. Ajoblanco (white gazpacho with almonds) is a Malaga speciality.
  • Fritura Malagueña: A mixed plate of lightly battered, fried seafood — calamares, anchovies, small fish. Try it at Casa Mira on the beach.
  • Malaga Wine: The province produces distinctive sweet wines under the Malaga DOC — particularly Pedro Ximénez. Order a glass of vino de Málaga with dessert.

Walking tip: Almost all of Malaga's historic attractions are within 15–20 minutes walk of each other in the compact old city. Wear comfortable shoes and start early — by 11 AM in summer, the city is already at full heat.

Day Trips from Malaga City

Malaga's position at the centre of the Costa del Sol makes it an excellent base for day trips:

  • Malaga to Granada: The Alhambra and Albaicín — the most popular day trip from Malaga (125 km, 1h 30m)
  • Malaga to Ronda: One of Andalusia's most dramatic hilltop cities, with the famous Puente Nuevo gorge bridge (100 km, 1h 20m)
  • Malaga to Nerja: Beautiful coastal town with the Cuevas de Nerja caves and Balcón de Europa (55 km, 55 min)
