Day Trip from Malaga to Ronda: Everything You Need to Know

Malaga VIP Transfers•Day Trip•7 min read

Ronda is arguably the most dramatic town in Andalusia. Perched on the edge of a sheer 100-metre gorge with its extraordinary 18th-century Puente Nuevo bridge spanning the void, it is unlike anywhere else in Spain. A day trip from Malaga to Ronda is one of the most popular excursions on the Costa del Sol — and one of the most rewarding.

How Far is Ronda from Malaga?

Malaga to Ronda is approximately 100 kilometres by road. The journey takes around 1 hour 20 minutes to 1 hour 40 minutes via the A-357 road, which passes through dramatic mountain scenery in its final section. The road up to Ronda is winding but well-maintained — mountain views are spectacular, but those prone to car sickness should sit up front.

If departing from Malaga Airport, the distance to Ronda is approximately 115 km, adding about 15–20 minutes to the journey.

Train — Malaga to Ronda (Scenic Route)

The Malaga to Ronda train is one of the most scenic rail journeys in Spain. Renfe operates services via the A-Andalus route through the Serranía de Ronda — the journey takes approximately 1 hour 50 minutes to 2 hours 15 minutes. Trains run 2–4 times daily; always check the Renfe website as schedules vary seasonally. Cost is approximately €13–20 per person.

The advantage of the train is the scenery — the line passes through dramatic gorges and viaducts that are impossible to appreciate from the motorway. The disadvantage is the limited daily schedule, which can restrict your time in Ronda.

Bus (Comes/Damas)

Bus services run from Malaga bus station to Ronda approximately 4–6 times daily, with journey times of around 1 hour 45 minutes to 2 hours. Cost is approximately €10–15 per person. Check the Comes website for current schedules.

What to See in Ronda

Puente Nuevo & El Tajo Gorge

The Puente Nuevo (New Bridge) is Ronda's defining landmark — an 18th-century stone arch spanning a 100-metre-deep gorge with a river at the bottom. The bridge took 42 years to build (1759–1793) and now houses a small museum in its central chamber. The views from both sides of the gorge are extraordinary. Go at sunrise or sunset for the most dramatic light.

Plaza de Toros (Bullring)

Ronda's Plaza de Toros is one of the oldest and most beautiful bullrings in Spain — the birthplace of modern bullfighting. The ring dates to 1784, and its arched arcades and ochre-coloured sand are far more elegant than the concrete stadiums in larger cities. The attached museum tells the history of the corrida with remarkable artefacts including matadors' costumes.

Old Town (Ciudad)

The original Moorish city of Ronda occupies the southern side of the gorge. The Arab baths are among the best-preserved in Spain; the Church of Santa María la Mayor was built on a mosque; and the narrow streets reward slow walking and exploration. The Mirador de Aldehuela offers the best elevated view of the Puente Nuevo.

Casa del Rey Moro Gardens

A 14th-century Moorish palace with terraced gardens descending into the gorge via a spectacular series of stone steps. The views from the bottom, looking up at the bridge, are stunning.
