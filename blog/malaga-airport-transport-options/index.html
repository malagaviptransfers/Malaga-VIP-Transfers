Malaga Airport Transport Options — Rental Cars, Taxi, Shuttle, Train & Private Transfer Compared

Malaga VIP Transfers•Transport Guide•12 min read

Arriving at Malaga Airport (AGP) and wondering how to get to your hotel? You have five main options: rental cars in Malaga Airport, metered taxis, the train, the bus, a shuttle transfer Malaga Airport, or a private transfer Malaga Airport. This guide compares every option honestly so you can make the right choice for your trip, group size, and budget.

Option 1: Rental Cars Malaga Airport

Rental Cars in Malaga Airport

Malaga Airport rental cars are available from all major providers — Hertz, Avis, Enterprise, Europcar, Sixt, and budget brands — in a dedicated multi-storey car park connected to Terminal 3. Car hire Malaga Airport is one of the most searched transport options for good reason: with a hire car, you have full independence to explore the Costa del Sol and Andalusia at your own pace. The AP-7 motorway connects directly to most major resorts.

ProsFull independence and flexibility · Access to inland villages · Good for 10+ day stays · Multiple providers competing on price
ConsDaily insurance and fuel costs add up · Parking at hotels is expensive · Toll charges on AP-7 · Need to navigate at arrival (tired, unfamiliar roads) · One-way drop fees if applicable

Our verdict on rental cars Malaga Airport: Rental cars make sense for self-catering stays of a week or more, or for travellers planning significant independent exploration. For resort hotel stays focused on relaxation, the daily cost of car hire, parking, insurance and fuel rarely justifies the expense against a pre-booked private transfer in and out.

One important note: searches for rental cars in Malaga Airport peak in summer when availability is limited. If you plan to hire, book weeks ahead — particularly July and August.

Option 2: Malaga Airport taxi

Malaga Airport Taxi Rank

Metered white taxis queue outside Malaga Airport Terminal 3 arrivals 24 hours a day. The malaga airport taxi service requires no booking and takes up to 4 passengers. The meter starts on a base tariff with an airport supplement. For destinations within 30 minutes of the airport (Torremolinos, city centre, Benalmadena), a malaga taxi is competitive. For Marbella and beyond, metered fares rise significantly.

ProsNo booking required · Available immediately · Competitive for short distances
ConsNo fixed price — meter runs · Can queue at peak times · No flight monitoring · For Marbella can cost €100+ on meter · No meet-and-greet service · No guaranteed vehicle quality

Option 3: Malaga Airport shuttle transfer

Shuttle Transfer Malaga Airport

A Malaga Airport shuttle transfer is a shared minibus that picks up multiple passengers and drops them at hotels across the Costa del Sol in sequence. Services depart at set times and stop at multiple resorts. The shuttle transfer Malaga is usually cheaper per person than a private car, making it popular with solo travellers on tight budgets.

ProsCheaper per person for solo travellers · No navigation required
ConsShared with strangers — no privacy · Multiple stops — journey can take 2–3× longer · Wait for other passengers before departing · Set timetables — may not suit late-night arrivals · Not door-to-door in same sense as private car

For families, groups of 2 or more, or anyone arriving tired after a long flight, a private airport transfer Malaga typically costs less than two shuttle tickets combined — without the shared-bus experience.

Option 4: Malaga Airport train

Malaga Airport to City Centre by Train

The C1 Cercanías train runs from Malaga Airport directly to Malaga city centre (María Zambrano station, then Malaga Centro-Alameda) every 20 minutes from approximately 06:30 to 23:30. The journey takes 11 minutes to María Zambrano. This is the fastest and cheapest way to get from Malaga Airport to Malaga city centre — costing around €2.70.

ProsVery cheap (€2.70) · Fast to Malaga centre (11 min) · No traffic delays
ConsOnly goes to Malaga city — not Costa del Sol resorts · Inconvenient with heavy luggage · No service to Marbella, Nerja, Benalmadena, Fuengirola · Last service ~23:30

The Malaga airport train to city centre is the clear winner if you're staying in Malaga city. For anywhere else on the Costa del Sol — Marbella, Nerja, Benalmadena, Estepona — the train does not serve those destinations, so you will need a taxi, shuttle, private transfer or hire car.

Option 5: Malaga Airport Bus

Airport Bus

Line A bus connects Malaga Airport to Malaga city centre (approximately 20 minutes, €3). The Portillo bus company also operates services to some Costa del Sol destinations, departing from Terminal 3. Journey times are significantly longer than private transfer due to stops and traffic.

ProsVery cheap (€3) · No booking required
ConsLimited destinations · Long journey times · Not practical with luggage · Limited evening/night services

Malaga Airport — key facts

Malaga airport code: AGP. Full name: Aeropuerto de Málaga-Costa del Sol (Pablo Ruiz Picasso Airport). Located 8 km from Malaga city centre, 45 km from Marbella, 55 km from Nerja. Spain's 4th busiest airport, serving approximately 22 million passengers per year. Terminal 3 handles all international arrivals and departures. The Malaga airport code AGP doesn't come from a specific district — MAL, MLG and MGA were already taken by other airports, so IATA assigned Malaga A and G from the city's name plus an available P (the popular Picasso-connection story is a myth that post-dates the code).
